# Australia in Motion

**Two decades of strategy, innovation and economic change — told through data.**

Australia in Motion is a scrolling data-story project exploring how Australia changed from 2000 onward, how organisations responded, and what strategic choices may matter next.

## Narrative approach

Each chapter is designed around four questions:

1. What changed?
2. Why did it change?
3. How did organisations respond?
4. What strategic or innovation lesson emerges?

The project intentionally differs from a dashboard. It uses a small number of strong charts, progressive scrolling, short editorial commentary and recurring **Strategy Lens** and **Innovation Lens** sections.

## Voices of Australia

The project includes a contributor stream called **Voices of Australia**.

People can submit a perspective on a chapter or turning point through the GitHub issue form. Submissions are reviewed before publication. Selected perspectives may be incorporated into the story alongside the underlying data and strategic interpretation.

This is intended to capture lived experience without presenting anecdote as statistical evidence.

## Chapter 1 data

The initial prototype uses selected ABS population milestones:

- June 2000: 19.157 million
- June 2010: 22.33 million
- June 2020: 25.687 million
- December 2025: 27.801 million

The final version of the chapter should use the complete official time series rather than only these anchor points.

## Primary sources

Australian Bureau of Statistics:
- Australian Demographic Statistics / Year Book Australia
- Population by Age and Sex, Regions of Australia
- National, state and territory population

Future chapters will also draw on sources including the Reserve Bank of Australia, Productivity Commission, Jobs and Skills Australia and other official Australian datasets where relevant.

## Principles

- Public data first
- Sources visible
- Facts separated from interpretation
- No fabricated contributor quotes
- Scenarios clearly distinguished from forecasts
- AI interpretation separated from official statistics
