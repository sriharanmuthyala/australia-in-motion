Chapter 01 · A Bigger Australia

The country changed scale.

Population is not the whole story of economic change. But it changes the size of almost every market, the demand for infrastructure, the labour pool and the pressure placed on housing and services.

2000
19.2m

At the start of the century, Australia had just over 19 million residents. The economy, cities and workforce were operating at a very different scale.

2010
22.3m

A decade later, the population had increased by more than three million. Growth was being shaped by both natural increase and overseas migration.

2020
25.7m

By June 2020, Australia had reached 25.7 million people — just as international border restrictions were about to disrupt one of the country’s major engines of population growth.

2025
27.8m

By the end of 2025, the population was close to 28 million. The strategic question was no longer simply how to grow, but how to add housing, infrastructure, skills and productivity fast enough to support that scale.

Strategy lens

Scale creates opportunity — and constraints.

A larger domestic market supports new demand, deeper labour pools and greater investment. But growth also exposes bottlenecks. Housing, transport, health, education and skills become strategic capacity questions, not merely background conditions.

Voices of Australia

The data tells us what changed. People tell us what it felt like.

We want this story to include perspectives from people who experienced these shifts inside businesses, communities and institutions.

A contributor might share what COVID changed inside their organisation, how the mining boom changed a regional economy, what remote work did to leadership, or how AI is changing the way their team works.

Selected contributions can be woven into future chapters alongside the data and the Strategy and Innovation lenses.
